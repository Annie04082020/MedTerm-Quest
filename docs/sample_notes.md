# MedTerm Quest sample notes

Original, compact study notes for the included Phase 1 question bank. These are demonstration materials, not imported course lectures. The section names below are the source references shown in the activities.

## Week 1 · Anatomical directions

In standard anatomical position, **medial** means toward the body's midline and **lateral** means away from it. **Anterior** describes the front of the human body; **ventral** also describes the front in this context.

**Proximal** and **distal** compare locations with respect to a limb's point of attachment: proximal is nearer, distal is farther. Fingers are distal to the wrist. **Superficial** describes a location nearer the body surface; **deep** describes one farther inside.

The combining vowel `o` often connects a root to another word part. The English word **heart** names the muscular organ that pumps blood. The mini-crossword uses `HEART`, `HEMO`, and `TACHY`, omitting the slash and hyphen.

## Week 1 · Directional terminology scenario

A superficial wound is near the body surface. “Superficial” describes position; the word alone does not establish injury severity.

## Week 2 · Feedback loops

Homeostatic regulation maintains internal variables within suitable ranges. A negative feedback response opposes a disturbance. Positive feedback reinforces a change; childbirth is an example, with delivery ending the reinforcing loop.

## Week 2 · Control system components

A **receptor** detects a change, a **control center** processes information and directs a response, and an **effector** performs the response. For engineering students, these correspond broadly to a sensor, controller, and actuator.

## Week 2 · Thermoregulation scenario

Sweat evaporation consumes thermal energy and removes heat from the body. It supports the negative feedback response to an elevated body temperature. Sweat must evaporate to provide this cooling effect.

## Week 3 · Blood vessels

Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry it toward the heart. Classification depends on direction, rather than oxygen content.

## Week 3 · Pulmonary circulation

In adult pulmonary circulation, the pulmonary arteries carry lower-oxygen blood from the right ventricle toward the lungs. This is an exception to the common association of arteries with oxygen-rich blood.

## Week 3 · Flow variables

**Heart rate (HR)** is the number of beats per minute. **Stroke volume (SV)** is the volume ejected by one ventricle per beat. **Cardiac output (CO)** is the volume pumped by one ventricle per minute.

## Week 3 · Cardiac output

`CO = HR × SV`. For example, `(90 beats/min) × (70 mL/beat) = 6300 mL/min = 6.3 L/min`.
